3D TOOL BUILDER DICTIONARY
Created by: Ms. Amina Qatan

Open index.html or publish all the files with GitHub Pages.

WHAT CHANGED
This standalone app is called 3D Tool Builder Dictionary. Curriculum Tools is a new tab with All three levels, Level 1, Level 2 and Level 3 in a dropdown. It contains 34 distinct tools, machines and relevant measuring instruments found in the available key-vocabulary and glossary lists across 15 curriculum units: 8 entries in Level 1, 30 in Level 2 and 13 in Level 3. Repeated terms share one model; the level totals overlap. General vocabulary, PPE, transport, generic words such as machinery, and standalone components are excluded from these lists. Search results show the tool and function without repeated unit destinations. Course tabs have not returned.

All 34 curriculum models have separate labelled parts to assemble (132 parts in total). Engine, alternator, cement mill, kiln and turbine now have their own models and function scenes. Every curriculum tool has a useful example sentence with a Hear sentence button. The existing Build a Workshop activity remains separate.

The page now has a playful pastel theme, bold rounded controls and three simple progress steps. The diagram stays above the parts tray. Sound and pronunciation start when the student first touches a part. When dragging near the top or bottom edge of a phone screen, the page scrolls to help reach the diagram. The held part stays above the finger. Students can also tap a part and then its target.

BUILD AND SEE THE FUNCTION
1. Open Curriculum Tools, choose a level, and select a tool or machine. You can also use the main search or tool picker.
2. Drag each part onto its shape, number or name, or tap a part and then its target.
3. Press Switch on for powered equipment or Show function for manual/passive items.
4. A function illustration shows the item's use or outcome. Working parts animate on applicable 3D models; the camera no longer rotates automatically as a substitute for the function.
5. Drag the completed model with one finger to rotate it, pinch with two fingers to zoom, or use Side, Other side, End view, Rear, Top and Bottom. The parts tray hides while exploring. Press Assemble to return.
6. Press Stop function to stop both the demonstration and its operational sound. Read or listen to the useful example sentence beneath the workbench.

Function examples include making a hole, driving a nail, cutting a workpiece, gripping, turning a fastener, mixing, pumping water, lifting, forming stitches, measuring, filtering air and converting energy. Functional diagrams and measurements are classroom examples, not live readings or real operating instructions.

SOUND AND VOICE
Effects and voice are enabled by default, with app volume set to 85%. Touch a part or tap Enable effects / Enable voice to start playback. There is no sound-test opening window. Labels speak when tapped, and words, functions and example sentences have Listen buttons. A Listen button restores app volume if the slider is at zero.

Powered tools and contact actions use representative sound effects; they are not recordings of a particular make or model. Normally silent devices remain silent. A clear installed British English voice is preferred at a measured pace. If no UK voice is available, another English voice is used, or the device is asked for en-GB, so unavailable voice-list metadata does not silently prevent the request. The app identifies the fallback; install an English (United Kingdom) device voice for a British accent. The drill's existing British recordings and click sounds remain built in. App volume and the device's Media volume must both be audible. Turning effects off stops operational sounds. Changing model or tab, resetting, or hiding the page stops the previous demonstration.

Reduced-motion settings pause automatic animation. Use Next step in the function panel to view the sequence. Sound and spoken explanations have independent controls.

BUILD A WORKSHOP
All 21 classroom workshop and laboratory layouts remain. Each room has six equipment items and retains its assembly progress while the page is open. Search for a workshop or lab by name. Chemistry, Physics and Fish Quality Control labs are included. Items excluded from the tool dictionary may remain as relevant equipment in these room layouts.

3D APPEARANCE
The simplified rotatable illustrations retain metal highlights, dark rubber, wood grain and soft shadows. Every curriculum item has three to six teaching parts. Additional equipment remains available inside the existing workshop layouts. Cutaways and assemblies explain vocabulary and function; they are not maintenance instructions.

UPDATE THE EXISTING GITHUB SITE
1. Unzip Workshop_Builder_GitHub_Ready.zip on your computer.
2. Open https://github.com/AminaQatan/Vocational-Tool-Builder-3D and choose main.
3. Choose Add file > Upload files.
4. Upload every file FROM INSIDE the extracted folder. index.html must be at the repository root.
5. Commit to main, replacing the matching files.
6. If needed: Settings > Pages > Deploy from a branch > main > /(root) > Save.
7. After deployment, refresh https://aminaqatan.github.io/Vocational-Tool-Builder-3D/ online. Close and reopen an installed copy after refreshing the website.

The repository URL remains the same even though the app's displayed title has changed.

INSTALL / SHARE
Open the published HTTPS link on your device. Use the browser's Install or Add to Home Screen option where available. Share the same link with students. The first successful visit caches the app for offline use. Availability of offline British speech depends on the device's installed voices. No account, microphone, API key or student data is required.

Verification covers all 34 curriculum assemblies and 132 parts, level filtering, useful sentences, function scenes, touch drop on names/numbers, wrong drops, cancellation, edge scrolling, rotation/pinch controls, pronunciation routing and offline assets. Actual mobile/smartboard speaker output, browser display and performance require testing on those devices.
